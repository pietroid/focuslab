# Why

The main problem I want to solve is organization of time. I have already tried everything. From pomodoro, simple todo lists, GTD methodology (Get Things Done), putting things on calendar, tracking time, etc.

And everything kind of worked, for quite a good time. However, after some weeks, I would find myself in a mess again. And I think this revels the nature of our cognitive limitations. Too few repetition, very flexible systems, chaos. Too much repetition, too much rigidity, we do not convince ourselves about the utility of that system, and chaos again.

So we don't need to exclude anything. Every method works. Every method has its moment. But the key thing is constant tracking, constant feedback loops, that thing about continuous improvement, etc. The thing is: how to build such a system?

So the sole focus of focus is plain simple: a continuous, ever adapting system, feedback-looped that ties to our internal clocks and our cognitive triggers to makes our life easier and makes we focus on the important stuff.

# What

Such a wide scope, and such tries to evolve the focus project, this won't be the first time, nor the last that I'm doing an experiment here. However, this time I have somethings in place that makes most of sense in our current context which is:

- Primary design concerned with Desktop/TV. I have been testing my TV as information centralizer for the last weeks and it's working good. Everyone in the house has information. We have space to put anything that we need.

- Secondary design concerned with Mobile app. We need to use mobile apps for two things mainly: User input and Notifications. However, we don't want to focus too much on overly complicated and fancy visualizations inside it. Anything too complex should be on the primary screen, TV.

- Calendar centric: Calendar views are the most straightforward time organization interface. Given that we have too much space for it we can tweak a calendar view putting in as much detail as we want.

- Flexibility: What turns me off on using a plain simple Google Calendar is flexibility. I want time slots. I want to put my todo lists there. I want to see visually my time to wake up, and to sleep. I want to move slots without breaking stuff.

- Triggers: In the era of event centric society, everything is a trigger to something. I want to enable as much as possible system to human triggers (push notifications in the right time, right place), and also human to system triggers (any input from human that drives the plan differently).

- Simplicity: This isn't an app for fancy animations or stuff (could be in the future!), but should be super neutral and yet powerful, (like yeah, notion) but different from notion, it has even more simplicity. It isn't an app for managers or for organizations, it's for people that just straightforward want to get things done, at the same time it provide room to put disorganized informations from the mind of people to structured and unified information, spread evenly on time.

# How

The how should be broken down in smaller tasks, but that is the overall:

- Calendar view: Build a customized calendar view that takes up the whole screen from left to right, top to bottom. It can be zoomed in or out and must show day to day side by side.
- Calendar view must have the basic capability of adding a new event on any place of its grid, and also recurrence.
- Calendar must support at least two people (again, our needs as a couple!), so two timelines in the same view.
- Calendar must support images, colors, etc - but this should be more for an improvement.
- Guiding principle: slots - usually fixed and recurring (but can be changed) - and tasks that fill those slots.
- Notifications (with sound) - for given important stuff.
